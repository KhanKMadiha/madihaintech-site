Articulate

Practise the conversations that matter.

An AI-powered speaking practice app combining personalised daily reading with realistic professional rehearsals.

Explore live demo (opens in a new tab) View GitHub (opens in a new tab)

The challenge and what I built

The challenge

The idea started with advice from a senior engineering manager that stuck with me: technical professionals need to communicate differently with customers, Engineering and executives. I wanted a way to practise those conversations deliberately rather than wait for a high-stakes situation.

What I built

I designed and built Articulate, an AI-powered speaking practice app that combines personalised daily reading, realistic workplace rehearsals, specific feedback and accessible reading options.

Two ways to practise

A short daily reading habit, and a place to rehearse the conversations that carry more weight.

Daily Practice

Each day Claude writes a fresh passage around your role, goals, focus areas and the speakers you admire. You read it aloud and get feedback on how you delivered it.

  • Personalised passages from your role, goals and interests
  • Recording with live transcription in the browser
  • Feedback on accuracy, fluency and pace
  • A Library of saved passages, daily streaks and optional reminders
  • A dyslexia-friendly reading mode

Rehearse

Practise the high-stakes conversations of a job out loud, in your own words, and get coached on how you came across.

  • Choose what you’re practising for, such as Product Support or tech sales
  • Choose who you’re speaking to, from a customer to a C-suite exec
  • Pick a scenario, such as a P1 incident update or a renewal at risk
  • Claude builds the person, the situation, likely pushback and talking points
  • An optional example answer, and a cue card while you speak
  • Scores for structure, value, sounding the part and confidence
  • Stronger alternative lines and a rewrite to read before you try again

Career pivot mode

Moving into a new role? Tell Articulate the role you’re pivoting into, such as tech sales, Customer Success, Solutions Engineering or Product Management, and your daily passages are written in the voice of someone already doing that job, so you practise its vocabulary and situations before interviews and calls.

How it works

Select a step to explore how a practice session works, in Daily Practice and in Rehearse.

Step 1 of 5

Choose a goal

In Daily Practice, your role, goals, focus areas and the speakers you admire shape each passage. In Rehearse, you choose what you’re practising for, who you’re speaking to and a scenario.

Step 2 of 5

Generate practice

Claude writes a fresh passage for the day, or builds a realistic person, situation and likely pushback, with the beats to hit and phrases to try.

Step 3 of 5

Speak aloud

Read the passage aloud, or answer in your own words with a cue card keeping the key beats in view. The browser transcribes as you speak.

Step 4 of 5

Review feedback

Daily Practice scores accuracy, fluency and pace. Rehearse scores structure, value, sounding the part and confidence, including whether your detail and tone fit the audience.

Step 5 of 5

Try again

See the lines that weakened your answer, stronger alternatives and a rewrite in a seasoned professional’s voice, then try again. Daily passages can be saved to your Library.

Why I designed it this way

Three decisions shaped how I built Articulate.

  • Make practice relevant

    Generic exercises are easy to skip. I wanted practice to reflect the conversations people actually have at work, so passages are written around your role and goals, and rehearsals are built on real moments like a P1 update or a renewal at risk.

  • Give specific feedback

    A score on its own doesn’t tell you what to change. Articulate points to the exact lines that weakened an answer and shows a stronger way to say them, so the next attempt is better.

  • Design accessibility into the experience

    While testing, I watched someone struggle to read a passage. That changed the project from adding an optional font to redesigning the reading experience, and it now shapes how passages are written too.
